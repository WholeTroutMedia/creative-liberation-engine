## 🧠 ATHENA Directive

> [!tip] Primary Directive
> Establish the Creative Liberation Engine as the definitive, sovereign 'Company Brain' for internal operations, leveraging advanced intelligence agents to automate context assembly, knowledge management, and proactive insights, thereby eliminating human wait time and ensuring complete, self-owned data sovereignty.

### Rationale

The 'Scout' project presents a compelling vision for an open-source company intelligence agent. To align with Creative Liberation Engine's Constitutional Laws, particularly Article I (Sovereignty), Article IV (Quality Standards), and Article IX (Ship Complete), we must either fully control or natively develop such a system. The core concept of an 'AI Operating System for Companies' directly supports Article XX (Zero human wait time) by automating knowledge and context retrieval.

## ⚡ Strategic Options

### 🟡 Deep Integration & Customization of Scout

Adopt Scout as a foundational layer, heavily customizing its context providers and core agent logic to align perfectly with Creative Liberation Engine's architecture and data sovereignty requirements. This involves forking, extending, and integrating Scout's codebase directly into our self-hosted infrastructure.

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Faster initial deployment of 'Company Brain' features due to existing codebase; leverages proven patterns (navigation over search, context providers); reduced development effort for common integrations (Slack, Drive). Cons: Potential for architectural impedance mismatch with Creative Liberation Engine's deeper systems; requires significant ongoing maintenance and customization effort to keep it aligned and prevent 'fork drift'; may not fully satisfy Article I (Sovereignty) if core design principles are not fully ours.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### ✅ Scout as Architectural Blueprint for Native Development

Treat Scout as a comprehensive reference architecture. Extract its core principles (navigation over search, modular context providers, agentic SQL, persistent memory) and re-implement them natively within the Creative Liberation Engine's existing framework. Develop our own 'Company Brain' from the ground up, inspired by Scout but fully owned and tailored.

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Full adherence to Article I (Sovereignty) and complete control over the entire stack; perfect architectural alignment with Creative Liberation Engine's long-term vision; allows for deeper optimization and integration with other Creative Liberation Engine components. Cons: Significantly higher initial development effort and time; requires replicating functionality that Scout already provides; risk of 'reinventing the wheel' if not carefully managed.
> **Recommendation:** `PREFERRED`

### 🟡 Focus on Advanced 'Navigation Over Search' Module

Isolate and prioritize the 'navigation over search' paradigm from Scout. Develop a dedicated, highly intelligent agent within the Creative Liberation Engine whose sole purpose is to navigate disparate internal knowledge sources (codebases, documentation, communication logs) using dynamic, agentic reasoning, rather than relying on static vector database ingestion. This module would be a core component of our eventual 'Company Brain.'

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Addresses a critical pain point (knowledge retrieval) with a proven, superior method; creates a powerful, reusable core component; lower initial scope than a full 'Company Brain.' Cons: Does not immediately deliver a complete 'Company Brain' solution (Article IV, IX); risks becoming a standalone component without clear integration path if not designed as part of a larger plan.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🟡 Develop an Creative Liberation Engine 'Context Provider Framework'

Inspired by Scout's modular ContextProvider approach, design and build a robust, standardized framework within the Creative Liberation Engine for creating and managing context providers. These providers would expose natural-language tools (query/update) to Creative Liberation Engine agents, abstracting away source-specific quirks. This framework would then be used to build internal integrations (e.g., for Creative Liberation Engine's own code, documentation, project management).

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Establishes a scalable and maintainable architecture for integrating diverse information sources; promotes modularity and reduces context pollution for agents; directly addresses a key architectural pattern from Scout. Cons: Framework development can be abstract and delay direct 'Company Brain' features; requires significant design upfront to ensure flexibility and robustness.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🔴 External Scout Instance for Rapid Prototyping/Benchmarking

Deploy a standalone instance of Scout (e.g., on Railway) for internal experimentation, benchmarking, and learning. Use it to validate the 'Company Brain' concept and gather insights into agentic workflows and information source integration, without immediately integrating it into the Creative Liberation Engine's core.

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Fastest way to gain practical experience with a 'Company Brain' system; low commitment, allowing for rapid learning and concept validation. Cons: Does not contribute directly to the Creative Liberation Engine's self-owned infrastructure (violates Article I); not a complete implementation (violates Article IV, IX); primarily a research tool, not a production strategy.
> **Recommendation:** `AVOID`

### 🟡 Prioritize Agentic SQL & Schema-on-Demand for Creative Liberation Engine Data Layer

Focus on implementing Scout's 'agentic SQL' and 'schema on demand' principles directly into the Creative Liberation Engine's persistent memory and data management layers. This would empower Creative Liberation Engine agents to dynamically interact with and evolve the internal data structures (like a self-building CRM/Wiki) based on observed information and needs.

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Enhances data flexibility and agent autonomy; directly supports the self-building aspect of a 'Company Brain'; addresses a core data management challenge for intelligent agents. Cons: Highly complex technical undertaking; requires robust database and agent interaction design; may be a prerequisite rather than a complete strategy on its own.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`
