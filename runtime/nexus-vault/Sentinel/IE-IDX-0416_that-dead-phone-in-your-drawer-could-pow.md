## 🧠 ATHENA Directive

> [!tip] Primary Directive
> Transform dormant consumer hardware into a sovereign, decentralized edge-compute swarm, liberating AI infrastructure from centralized cloud dependencies while enforcing strict local-first data governance.

### Rationale

True sovereignty (Article I) requires independence from centralized compute monopolies. By weaponizing the latent processing power, memory, and sensors of discarded mobile devices, we create a resilient, distributed, and entirely owned infrastructure grid. This aligns deeply with our edge-compute mandate and biometric data sovereignty (Article XXIV), as local devices process data entirely behind the organizational firewall without cloud export.

## ⚡ Strategic Options

### ✅ The Sovereign Swarm: Distributed Edge Inference

Deploy a lightweight, containerized runtime (e.g., via Termux or custom Android ROMs) to pool the NPU/GPU/CPU resources of old phones into a unified inference cluster. The swarm will run highly quantized (e.g., 2-bit or 4-bit GGUF) foundation models, slicing layers across devices to execute inference collectively.

> **Tradeoffs:** High network latency between nodes compared to PCIe/NVLink; thermal throttling and battery degradation on older devices; complex orchestration required to handle node dropouts.
> **Recommendation:** `PREFERRED`

### 🟡 The Memory Spine Shards: Distributed Vector Storage

Repurpose the flash storage and RAM of legacy devices to act as a decentralized, highly redundant KV store and vector database for the KEEPER memory spine. Each phone acts as a shard, holding localized context or specific constitutional embeddings.

> **Tradeoffs:** Older eMMC storage is prone to failure and slow I/O; requires aggressive redundancy protocols (e.g., erasure coding) to prevent data loss when a device inevitably dies.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🟡 The Sensorium Grid: Multimodal Ingestion Network

Utilize the high-quality cameras, microphones, and accelerometers of dead phones as sovereign environmental ingestion nodes. They act as the physical 'eyes and ears' of the Creative Liberation Engine, processing biometric and environmental data strictly on-device (Article XXIV) before sending sanitized, vectorized insights to the main NAS.

> **Tradeoffs:** Continuous power delivery required; physical mounting and thermal management in deployment environments; potential ethical/privacy hurdles requiring strict COMPASS/LEX oversight.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🟡 The Fallback Hive: Redundant Orchestration Layer

Run lightweight agent dispatchers (SWITCHBOARD/RELAY) and constitutional validation checks (LEX) on mobile devices. If the primary NAS experiences downtime, the mobile hive maintains the system's heartbeat, routing, and critical state preservation until recovery.

> **Tradeoffs:** State synchronization complexity across intermittent, low-power nodes; potential split-brain scenarios during network partitions.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🔴 The Ephemeral Training Farm: Federated Fine-Tuning

Utilize idle cycles on the mobile grid to perform federated learning or LoRA fine-tuning for specialized agent skills. Each phone computes a small batch of gradients locally and syncs weights back to the primary NAS.

> **Tradeoffs:** Mobile SoCs are vastly inefficient for backpropagation compared to modern datacenter GPUs; time-to-completion for training jobs may violate Article XX (Zero human wait time).
> **Recommendation:** `AVOID`

### 🔴 The Immutable Ledger Nodes: Cryptographic Enclaves

Leverage the hardware-backed secure enclaves (e.g., TrustZone, Secure Enclave) of old phones to cryptographically sign, timestamp, and validate constitutional compliance checks, creating a physically decentralized audit trail.

> **Tradeoffs:** Extremely difficult to interface with proprietary secure enclaves on legacy, locked-down OEM hardware; reverse-engineering violates Article XXV (Integration Over Reverse-Engineering).
> **Recommendation:** `AVOID`
