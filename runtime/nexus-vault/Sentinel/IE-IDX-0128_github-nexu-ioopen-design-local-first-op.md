## 🧠 ATHENA Directive

> [!tip] Primary Directive
> Establish the Creative Liberation Engine as the sovereign platform for generative design, leveraging a local-first, agent-driven, and artifact-centric architecture inspired by the open-source ethos to deliver complete, high-quality design outputs with unparalleled flexibility and control.

### Rationale

The `open-design` project demonstrates a powerful paradigm shift in AI-driven design by prioritizing local execution, agent autonomy, and tangible artifact generation over proprietary cloud-locked solutions. This aligns perfectly with the Creative Liberation Engine's Constitutional Laws, particularly Article I (Sovereignty) and Article IX (Ship Complete). By integrating or adapting these principles, we can empower the Creative Liberation Engine to not only generate code but also to architect and produce complete, brand-grade design assets, significantly expanding its capabilities and reinforcing its self-hosted, owned solution philosophy.

## ⚡ Strategic Options

### 🟡 Creative Liberation Engine Design Core: Open Design Fork

Directly fork and integrate `nexu-io/open-design` into the Creative Liberation Engine's architecture, customizing it to seamlessly leverage Creative Liberation Engine's agents (BOLT for code, AURORA for architecture, KEEPER for design systems, etc.) as the underlying 'coding agents.' This means modifying Open Design to use our internal agent communication protocols and potentially replacing its daemon with our own, while retaining its UI, skill system, and artifact generation capabilities.

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Rapid deployment of a robust design capability; leverages existing, proven open-source work; benefits from `open-design`'s community and updates; aligns with BYOK/model-agnostic approach. Cons: Potential for significant refactoring to align with Creative Liberation Engine's internal agent architecture and Constitutional Laws; risk of inheriting `open-design`'s technical debt or architectural constraints; may lead to a less 'native' Creative Liberation Engine experience if not deeply integrated.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### ✅ Native Creative Liberation Engine Design Blueprint: Replicate & Enhance

Design and construct a bespoke 'Design Blueprint' module within the Creative Liberation Engine. This module will natively implement the local-first, agent-driven, artifact-centric, and skill-based design workflow observed in `open-design`, but fully optimized for the Creative Liberation Engine's unique agent ecosystem and architectural principles. It will involve a dedicated, end-to-end build under AURORA's guidance, ensuring complete sovereignty and adherence to all Constitutional Laws.

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Uncompromised architectural control and complete ownership (Article I: Sovereignty); seamless integration with Creative Liberation Engine's existing agents and future expansion; no external dependencies or inherited technical debt; superior long-term maintainability and quality (Article IV: Quality Standards). Cons: Requires a significant, dedicated development effort and potentially longer initial time-to-market compared to direct integration; demands substantial resource allocation from AURORA and BOLT for a ground-up implementation.
> **Recommendation:** `PREFERRED`

### 🟡 Autonomous Design Agent Environment

Focus on developing a highly autonomous 'Design Agent' within the Creative Liberation Engine that mimics the 'agent-as-teammate' paradigm. This agent, potentially a specialized BOLT variant or a new agent, would have full filesystem access, the ability to run external CLIs (similar to `open-design`'s PATH scanning), interact with design systems (from KEEPER), and generate artifacts. The UI would provide a live feedback loop, showing the agent's plan, tool calls, and sandboxed previews.

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Deeply integrates with the Creative Liberation Engine's agent philosophy; provides maximum flexibility for future agent-driven tasks beyond design; fosters true agent autonomy; aligns with the 'Zero human wait time' principle by automating design processes. Cons: Requires significant foundational work on agent capabilities (filesystem, CLI execution, real-time feedback); may initially lack the breadth of design skills and systems present in `open-design`; steep learning curve for users if not well-abstracted.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🟡 Modular Design Services API

Abstract the design generation capabilities into a set of internal, modular microservices accessible via an API within the Creative Liberation Engine. These services would handle specific design tasks (e.g., 'generate_prototype,' 'create_pitch_deck,' 'render_video'), each leveraging appropriate internal or external LLMs/agents and design systems. `open-design`'s features would serve as a blueprint for the functionality and quality of these services.

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Highly scalable and maintainable; promotes reusability of design components across different Creative Liberation Engine modules; allows for easy integration with future design tools or agents; aligns with a robust architectural approach. Cons: Requires careful definition of service boundaries and APIs; may lose some of the 'local-first' immediacy if not designed with local execution in mind; initial overhead in developing and orchestrating multiple services.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🟡 Creative Liberation Engine UI/UX: Live Artifacts & Interactive Previews

Prioritize the adoption of `open-design`'s UI/UX patterns, specifically the 'streaming-artifact loop,' 'sandboxed-iframe preview,' 'live agent panel (todos + tool calls + interruptible generation),' and interactive forms for design briefing. This would significantly enhance the Creative Liberation Engine's user experience for any generative task, not just design, making the process more transparent, interactive, and efficient.

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Immediate and tangible improvement in user experience; increases user engagement and trust in agent outputs; reduces iteration time; applicable across all Creative Liberation Engine generative functions. Cons: Primarily a UI/UX initiative, which might not immediately address the underlying generative capabilities; requires significant front-end development effort; needs robust backend support for streaming and sandboxing.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🔴 Strategic Alliance: Open Design Collaboration

Instead of forking or replicating, establish a strategic partnership with the `nexu-io/open-design` team. Contribute to their project, influencing its roadmap to align with Creative Liberation Engine's needs, and build a dedicated integration layer that allows Creative Liberation Engine agents to seamlessly invoke `open-design`'s functionalities. This would treat `open-design` as a specialized external design engine.

> **Tradeoffs:** Pros: Access to `open-design`'s expertise and ongoing development without full ownership burden; potentially faster time to market for design features; fosters open-source collaboration. Cons: Reliance on an external project's roadmap and stability; less control over the core architecture and adherence to Creative Liberation Engine's Constitutional Laws (Article I: Sovereignty); potential for conflicting priorities or architectural mismatches.
> **Recommendation:** `AVOID`
