## 🧠 ATHENA Directive

> [!tip] Primary Directive
> Decouple cognitive planning from mechanical execution by establishing an asymmetric, multi-model DAG architecture where premium intelligence dictates strategy and commoditized intelligence handles implementation.

### Rationale

In accordance with Article XXIII (LLMs Are Components, Not Architecture), we must treat models as interchangeable intelligence sources. Using a high-tier model (e.g., Gemini 1.5 Pro) exclusively for high-context auditing and DAG (Directed Acyclic Graph) generation allows us to leverage its reasoning capabilities where it matters most. Routing the resulting well-defined, isolated tasks to cheaper, faster, or locally hosted tier-3 models (e.g., Llama 3 8B, Gemini Flash) maximizes parallel execution, reduces latency, and aligns with our sovereign infrastructure goals without compromising the quality of the final output.

## ⚡ Strategic Options

### ✅ Asymmetric DAG Orchestrator (The Hivemind)

A top-tier model audits the codebase and generates a strict, dependency-mapped JSON task graph (DAG). The Creative Liberation Engine's dispatch system then routes individual leaf nodes to specialized, smaller local/cheap models based on the specific capability required for each node.

> **Tradeoffs:** Requires a highly robust orchestration and state-management spine to handle dependencies and merge conflicts. However, it maximizes cost-efficiency, enables massive parallelization, and perfectly embodies the Creative Liberation Engine architecture.
> **Recommendation:** `PREFERRED`

### 🟡 Test-Driven Cascade (The Validator Loop)

The premium model writes both the architectural plan and a comprehensive suite of strict unit/integration tests. Cheaper models are then tasked with writing the code, iterating in a closed loop until the premium model's tests pass.

> **Tradeoffs:** Increases execution time and compute cycles due to potential iteration loops by the smaller models. However, it absolutely guarantees Article IV (Quality Standards) and Article IX (No MVPs) compliance.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🟡 Continuous Background Auditor (Idle-Compute Sweeper)

The high-capability model continuously audits the codebase asynchronously during off-peak hours, generating a persistent backlog of micro-improvement tickets. Cheaper, local models pick up and execute these tasks silently in the background.

> **Tradeoffs:** Requires complex git-state management to avoid merge conflicts with active human/agent workstreams. Excellently fulfills Article XX (Zero human wait time) by making improvements an ambient, continuous process.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🟡 Context Compression Pipeline (The Golden Prompt)

Instead of just writing a plan, the premium model is used to compress the necessary codebase context into a highly dense, optimized 'golden prompt'. Cheaper models with smaller context windows use this prompt to perform tasks with tier-1 accuracy.

> **Tradeoffs:** Risk of critical context loss during the compression phase. If successful, it drastically reduces token costs and latency for the worker models.
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🟡 Sovereign Local Swarm (Air-Gapped Tiering)

Strictly enforce Article I (Sovereignty) by using a locally hosted 70B+ model for the audit and planning phase, and local 8B models for the execution phase. Zero cloud API dependency.

> **Tradeoffs:** Bound entirely by the NAS/Workstation's local hardware constraints and VRAM limits, potentially bottlenecking the audit phase. Maximizes biometric and data sovereignty (Article XXIV).
> **Recommendation:** `VIABLE`

### 🔴 Human-Gated Execution (The Approval Bottleneck)

The high-capability model audits the codebase and pauses the system, waiting for human approval on the generated plan before triggering the cheaper models for execution.

> **Tradeoffs:** Directly violates Article XX (Zero human wait time). Introduces manual friction and slows the momentum of the Creative Liberation Engine. Useful only for highly destructive/sensitive refactors.
> **Recommendation:** `AVOID`
